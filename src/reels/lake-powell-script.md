# Narration script — Lake Powell reel

DRAFT for Harvey to rewrite in his own voice. Short video (~60 sec, about 190
spoken words). Six beats (cold open + 5). Read the line, then tap the right side
of the screen to advance to the next beat. Keep it calm and even — the map does
the work.

Every sentence is followed by the ID of the claim it rests on, in
`dossiers/lake-powell.md` (the source record). Delete the `[C#]` tags when you
record. **Row C7 is an arithmetic inference** — see the notes at the bottom.

Policy note: this script says where and what, from the documents. It does not
argue for or against the policy.

---

**Beat 1 — cold open**
*(on screen: "8 feet above the target"; gauge shows the lake and the 3,510 line; chip "As of Sep 30, 2026")*
> Lake Powell is eight feet above its operating target. [C3, C5, C7] As of September 30, it stood at 3,518 feet, and Reclamation's new rules try to hold it at 3,510 or higher. [C3, C5]

**Beat 2 — two more lines**
*(on screen: "3,500 and 3,490"; the 3,500 and 3,490 lines appear under the target)*
> Below that, at 3,500 feet, the rules call for consultation and extra protective steps. [C6] At 3,490, the minimum power pool at Glen Canyon Dam, water can only pass through the river outlet works. [C4]

**Beat 3 — the map**
*(on screen: "Seven states, two lakes")*
> The Colorado River provides water for more than 40 million people, and hydropower for seven states. [C18] Powell, behind Glen Canyon Dam, and Lake Mead, behind Hoover Dam, hold it back. [C16]

**Beat 4 — who gets cut**
*(on screen: "1.25 million acre-feet a year"; Arizona, California, Nevada in red with amounts)*
> In 2027 and 2028, deliveries to Arizona, California and Nevada drop by 1.25 million acre-feet a year. [C9] Under the Lower Basin states' proposed sharing agreement, Arizona gives up 760,000 acre-feet, California 440,000, and Nevada 50,000. [C10]

**Beat 5 — upstream**
*(on screen: "Voluntary only"; Upper Division states in slate)*
> Reclamation's documents require no cut from the four Upper Division states. They can contribute voluntarily, up to 200,000 acre-feet a year. [C14, C21]

**Beat 6 — closer**
*(on screen: "Eight feet above the target"; chip "As of Sep 30, 2026")*
> The seven states didn't reach a deal, so Interior set the rules. [C19, C1] Reclamation says it will adjust releases through April to try to keep Powell at 3,510 or above. [C8] As of September 30, it was eight feet above. [C3, C7]

### Timing guide
Roughly 9–11 seconds per beat. Beat 1 has the hook in its first sentence:
"Lake Powell is eight feet above its operating target", with the number already
on screen. Let the map settle after each tap before starting the next line.
Lake Mead is on the map for context but is not in this cut; its levels and
triggers (C15, C17, C23) are in the source record if you want a Mead beat back.

### Fact-check before this goes public
- **Refresh the Powell level.** 3,518.08 ft is Reclamation's September 30, 2026
  daily row, and that page says its automatic reports "have not been checked for
  errors". Open https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/hourly/levels.html, read the
  current number, and change Beats 1, 2 and 6 (script, the date chips and sub-text,
  and the `LEVELS`/`ASOF` lines in the HTML file) if it has moved. "Eight feet" is
  subtraction from that number (C7).
- **3,510 is a target / buffer, not a floor.** The Reclamation press release
  calls 3,510 a "minimum elevation". The Operating Guidelines call it an
  operational buffer above a protected 3,500 ft. The script uses the Guidelines'
  wording and never calls 3,510 a minimum.
- **The state split is conditional (C10).** 760,000 / 440,000 / 50,000 applies
  "if the Lower Basin States implement their proposed sharing agreement". If they
  don't, the Secretary decides the split under existing law. Both Beat 4 and its
  on-screen text say so.
- **Division vs Basin.** The map colours whole states as "Upper Division" and
  "Lower Division", as the 1922 Colorado River Compact defines them (C21). The
  Reclamation press release says "Lower Basin States" for the same three states;
  the script follows the press release wording in Beat 4 and the Compact wording
  elsewhere. Under the Compact, "Basin" is a geographic term that cuts across
  state lines (parts of Arizona and New Mexico are in both), so only "Division"
  is accurate for whole-state colours.
- **Terms to explain in your own words:** acre-foot (a unit of water volume),
  apportioned, water year (starts Oct 1).
- **Reservoir outlines are full-pool shapes** from a generalized public-domain
  dataset, not today's shoreline. The Glen Canyon Dam point (OpenStreetMap) sits
  about 1 km outside the generalized Powell outline, so the dot may look slightly
  detached from the lake.

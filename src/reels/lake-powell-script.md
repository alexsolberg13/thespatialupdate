# Narration script — Lake Powell reel

DRAFT for Harvey to rewrite in his own voice. Short video (~60–70 sec). Seven
beats (cold open + 6). Read the line, then tap the right side of the screen to
advance to the next beat. Keep it calm and even — the map does the work.

Every sentence is followed by the ID of the claim it rests on, in
`dossiers/lake-powell.md` (the source record). Delete the `[C#]` tags when you
record. **Rows C7 and C12 are arithmetic inferences** — see the "Fact-check"
notes at the bottom.

The map (state outlines, reservoirs, dams) is described in the same record,
"Geometry" section. The page is `src/reels/lake-powell.html`.

Policy note: this script says where and what, from the documents. It does not
argue for or against the policy.

---

**Beat 1 — cold open**
*(on screen: "28 feet")*
> Twenty-eight feet. [C3, C4, C7] That's how far Lake Powell sits above 3,490 feet, the minimum power pool at Glen Canyon Dam. [C3, C4, C7]

**Beat 2 — the map**
*(on screen: "Seven states, two lakes")*
> The Colorado River provides water for more than 40 million people, and hydropower for seven states. [C18] Two reservoirs hold it back: Lake Powell, behind Glen Canyon Dam, and Lake Mead, behind Hoover Dam. [C16]

**Beat 3 — Lake Powell**
*(on screen: "3,518 feet")*
> On September 30, Powell stood at 3,518 feet. [C3] The new rules try to hold it at 3,510 or higher, to protect a 3,500-foot minimum. [C5] That's about eight feet of room above the target. [C7]

**Beat 4 — Lake Mead**
*(on screen: "1,038 feet")*
> That same day, Lake Mead stood at about 1,038 feet. [C15] If it's projected to fall below 1,000, the record calls for steps to protect the dam. [C17]

**Beat 5 — who gets cut**
*(on screen: "1.25 million acre-feet a year")*
> In 2027 and 2028, deliveries to Arizona, California and Nevada drop by 1.25 million acre-feet a year. [C9] If the states follow their own sharing plan, Arizona gives up 760,000 acre-feet, California 440,000, and Nevada 50,000. [C10]

**Beat 6 — by how much**
*(on screen: "27%, 10%, 17%")*
> Measured against what each state was apportioned before, that's about 27 percent for Arizona, 10 for California, and 17 for Nevada. [C12] The four Upper Basin states aren't part of that cut. The framework only provides for voluntary contributions from them. [C14, C21]

**Beat 7 — closer**
*(on screen: "Eight feet above the target")*
> The seven states didn't reach a deal, so Interior set the rules. [C19] Powell is expected to start the water year between 3,540 and 3,510 feet. [C8] Releases will be adjusted through April to try to keep it at 3,510 or above. [C8]

---

### Timing guide
Roughly 8–10 seconds per beat. Beat 1 is short on purpose: "Twenty-eight feet."
should land in the first two seconds, with the number already on screen. Let the
map settle after each tap before starting the next line. Total ≈ 60–70 sec
(about 215 spoken words).

### Tighter cut (~45 sec)
Drop Beat 4 (Lake Mead) and fold Beat 6's second half into Beat 5. The Lake
Powell question still stands without Mead; Mead is on the map for context.

### Fact-check before this goes public
- **Refresh the two elevations.** 3,518.08 ft (Powell) and 1,037.80 ft (Mead)
  are Reclamation's September 30, 2026 daily row. Open
  https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/hourly/levels.html, read the current
  numbers, and change Beats 1, 3, 4 and 7 (script and the `LEVELS` line in the
  HTML file) if they have moved. The "28 feet" and "eight feet" figures are
  subtraction from those numbers.
- **"28 feet" and "eight feet" (C7) are arithmetic, not a stated finding.**
  They are the elevation of the lake surface above two thresholds. They are not
  a prediction that the lake will drop or that anything fails. The documents
  call 3,490 ft the "minimum power pool", with "impacts to hydropower" below it
  — not a shutdown.
- **3,510 is a target, not a floor.** The Reclamation press release calls
  3,510 a "minimum elevation". The Operating Guidelines call it a buffer above a
  protected minimum of 3,500 ft. The script follows the Guidelines.
- **The state split is conditional (C10).** The 760,000 / 440,000 / 50,000 split
  applies "if the Lower Basin States implement their proposed sharing
  agreement". If they don't, the Secretary decides the split under existing law.
- **The percentages (C12) are computed by us**, from the new apportionments
  plus the reductions. The documents don't state them.
- **Reservoir outlines are full-pool shapes** from a generalized public-domain
  dataset, not today's shoreline. The dam points are Wikipedia coordinates.

# Narration script — Data center votes reel

DRAFT for Harvey to rewrite in his own voice. Short video (about 70 sec, about 200
spoken words). Six beats (cold open + 5). Read the line, then tap the right side
of the screen to advance to the next beat. Keep it calm and even. The map does
the work.

Every sentence is followed by the ID of the claim it rests on, in
`dossiers/data-center-votes.md` (the source record). Delete the `[C#]` tags when you
record. **Row C1 is a count of the five decisions below.**

Policy note: this script says where and what, from local reporting. It does not argue
for or against data centers or for or against any of these decisions.

---

**Beat 1 — cold open**
*(on screen: "Five votes in three days"; five pins, blue for approved and amber for the rest; chip "Sep 28 to 30, 2026")*
> Five local governments voted on data centers between September 28 and 30. [C1] Two approved a data center or the land for one. [C2, C5] One rejected a zoning change, one banned data centers and one extended a pause. [C11, C9, C14]

**Beat 2 — Cheyenne**
*(on screen: "1,260 acres added for a data center")*
> In Cheyenne, Wyoming, the city council voted 8 to 2 to bring about 1,260 acres of Cox Ranch into the city for a data center. [C2] SkyBox Data Centers and ViaWest Group plan to build it. [C3] The city already has ten data centers operating, five under construction and nine in development. [C4]

**Beat 3 — Kline Township**
*(on screen: "Amazon's 350-acre campus is approved")*
> In Kline Township, Pennsylvania, supervisors voted unanimously to approve a 350-acre Amazon campus near Route 309 and Interstate 81. [C5] The approval requires blast surveys of nearby homes before and after construction, and noise testing once it's built. [C6] Amazon still needs township zoning permits and state environmental clearances. [C7]

**Beat 4 — Lovejoy**
*(on screen: "A $1 billion plan, then a ban")*
> In Lovejoy, Georgia, the developer withdrew a $1 billion data center plan near two schools in August. [C8] On September 28 the city banned data centers. [C9] The mayor says the developers are now pursuing a residential project on the site. [C10]

**Beat 5 — Mercer County**
*(on screen: "Zoning next to a coal plant rejected")*
> In Mercer County, Kentucky, the fiscal court, the county's governing body, voted unanimously against zoning that would have allowed very large data centers next to the E.W. Brown coal plant. [C11] No specific data center had been proposed there. [C12] The rules go back to the planning commission. [C13]

**Beat 6 — Palm Springs**
*(on screen: "A pause through August 2028")*
> Palm Springs, California, extended its pause on data center applications through August 25, 2028. [C14] The city has no rules yet on where they can go, how loud they can be, or how much power and water they can use. [C15] Staff say the council would lift the pause once those rules are written. [C16]

---

### Timing guide
Roughly 10-12 seconds per beat. Beat 1 has the hook in its first sentence ("Five local
governments voted on data centers between September 28 and 30"), with the number already on
screen. Let the map settle after each tap before starting the next line.

### Fact-check before this goes public
- **Pins mark the town (or the plant), not the project site.** Four pins are Census place or
  township centres; the Mercer pin is the E.W. Brown plant (OpenStreetMap). Cox Ranch is west of
  Roundtop Road, outside the old Cheyenne city limits; Kline's site is near Route 309 and I-81.
- **This is five votes, not every vote that week.** Edgewater, Florida's ban was passed on
  September 14 (so it is not in the set), a newsletter's mention of a Posey County, Indiana
  approval was later corrected by local reporting (nothing was approved), and Warren County,
  Ohio's moratorium was only seen in a newsletter. See the dossier.
- **"Rejected zoning" in Mercer County is not a rejected project.** No data center had been
  proposed at the site; the vote sent the zoning rules back to the planning commission.
- **Lovejoy's $1 billion** comes from the AJC; the Atlanta News First report gives the size
  (3.5 million square feet, 15 buildings, 1.25 gigawatts) but not the dollar figure.
- **Kline's vote** was at a special meeting "Wednesday" (September 30); the local reports give no
  date on the page, so the date comes from the WNEP posting time.
- **Cheyenne's vote was an annexation and rezoning**, not approval of a built project (the buyers intend to build one). The legend's "Approved" covers both it and Kline's campus approval.
- **The reel shows five votes, not all that week.** The roundup lists more.
- **Terms to explain in your own words:** annexation, moratorium, hyperscale, fiscal court.

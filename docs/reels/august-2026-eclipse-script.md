# Narration script — The August 2026 Eclipse reel

Short video (~45 sec). Six beats. Read the line, then tap the right side of the
screen to advance to the next beat. Keep it calm and even — the map does the work.

Everything here traces to the story's claim ledger:
`/stories/august-2026-eclipse/sources/`.

---

**Beat 1 — cold open**
*(on screen: "The August 2026 Eclipse")*
> On one evening this August, the Moon's shadow crosses the top of the world and comes down on Europe. Whether you see it depends on where you're standing.

**Beat 2 — the band**
*(on screen: "A line across the Earth")*
> The total eclipse is only visible from inside a strip barely 294 kilometres wide. Step outside it, and you get a partial — the Sun bitten into, but never gone.

**Beat 3 — the Arctic**
*(on screen: "Greenland and Iceland")*
> The shadow lands first in the Arctic afternoon — across eastern Greenland, then clipping the western edge of Iceland. Reykjavík barely makes it: about 59 seconds.

**Beat 4 — Spain at sunset**
*(on screen: "Landfall at sunset")*
> Then it races down the Atlantic to northern Spain, arriving right at sunset, the Sun just 11 degrees off the horizon. It's the first total eclipse over mainland Spain in over a century.

**Beat 5 — the near-misses**
*(on screen: "So close")*
> And here's the cruel part. Madrid and Barcelona, the two biggest cities, will see the Sun ninety-nine point nine percent covered — and still miss it. On the wrong side of the line by a sliver.

**Beat 6 — the line**
*(on screen: "Which side are you on?")*
> One narrow path, from the Arctic to a Spanish sunset. The whole show comes down to a single line drawn across the Earth — and which side of it you happen to be standing on.

### Timing guide
Roughly 6–8 seconds per beat. Let the map settle after each tap before you start
the next line. Total ≈ 40–50 seconds.

visible only from a strip barely 294 kilometres wide — step outside it and the

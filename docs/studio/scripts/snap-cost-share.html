Beat 121 words · 8 sec

41 states and D.C. are over 6%

Forty-one states and Washington, D.C. had SNAP error rates above 6 percent in fiscal 2025. SNAP is the food stamp program.

Beat 228 words · 11 sec

Too high and too low both count

USDA says the error rate measures how accurately states set eligibility and benefit amounts. Overpayments and underpayments both count. Of the national 10.62 percent, 9.28 points are overpayments.

Beat 330 words · 12 sec

Access and speed aren't counted

Errors of $57 or less aren't counted. The Food Research and Action Center, a hunger-focused nonprofit, says the rate also leaves out barriers to participation, processing speed and program abuse.

Beat 448 words · 19 sec

States pay more at higher error rates

Starting as soon as October 2027, that rate sets what a state pays. States under 6 percent pay nothing. At 6 to 8 percent a state covers 5 percent of its benefit costs, at 8 to 10 it covers 10, and at 10 or higher it covers 15.

Beat 526 words · 10 sec

Alaska leads at 23.15%

Alaska is highest, at 23.15 percent, nearly four times the 6 percent threshold. Washington, D.C. is at 18.66. New Mexico, the next state, is at 16.81.

Beat 624 words · 10 sec

Payments start in fiscal 2028

For fiscal 2028, a state can use its 2025 rate or its 2026 rate. USDA will likely release the 2026 rates in June 2027.
